## The three full pitches

Three meaningfully different angles. Same product, three rooms.

### Option A — The Founder Story (~85 seconds)

**Best for:** investor pitches, podcasts, founder intros, stage talks, the "About" page. Anywhere personal credibility lands the trust.

> In every era of work I've sat through, the same humble tool quietly did the heavy lifting.
>
> When I was in the strategy department of ProSiebenSat.1, executing the board's digital strategy, the most important instrument we used wasn't a slide deck or a dashboard. It was an **ActionList**. Every Monday morning, we tracked its state.
>
> When I founded my own companies, our leadership meetings ran off the state of an action list. When I built product development teams, we broke product visions into feature lists, sprint lists, technical task lists, go-live checklists. When I started working with AI agents, I organized cross-session work with lists. And in personal life — birthday parties, relocations, weekly groceries, elderly care for my parents — the list is still doing the heavy lifting.
>
> **Lists drive how we work. Lists drive how we live.**
>
> But the AI era arrived without one. Your Claude Code todos die in 24 hours. Notion was built before AI existed. Linear retrofitted it. The list — humanity's most universal primitive of getting things done — hasn't yet entered the AI age.
>
> **And it's needed more than ever.** Work is fragmenting differently now — the same task flows between humans and agents in an hour, across multiple vendors, some finishing in seconds, others running overnight. Decisions get made along the way. **No single tool captures the whole picture.** Linear sees engineering. Notion sees docs. Slack sees messages. Claude sees prompts. Each gives you a slice. Synthesis falls on the human, who pays the cognitive tax of stitching it together by hand. **A universal list isn't a nice-to-have anymore. It's how you steer.**
>
> **Blitzlist is that universal list — built for the AI era.** AI-first from the foundation. Shared between you, your team, your AI agents, and your customers through MCP. Persistent across sessions. Tracks progress automatically. Draws your attention where it's needed. Open-source. Self-hostable on Cloudflare in three minutes.
>
> Lists drove every era of human work. The AI era was missing theirs. **But not anymore.**

### Option B — The Three-Frictions Pitch (~70 seconds)

**Best for:** cold outreach, demo openers, technical audiences, anyone who's already lived the pain.

> If you've spent a week working with AI agents, you've felt three things break down.
>
> **First: your AI's memory dies.** The todo list inside your Claude Code session is gone in 24 hours. The context you spent an hour building — gone. Every morning, you re-explain.
>
> **Second: your artifacts don't travel.** To get Claude to read a PPT, you screenshot it. To share Claude's output with a teammate, you copy-paste it into Slack. To get a designer's mockup into your AI session, you download and re-upload. The work happens in the gaps between tools, where humans do the manual carrying.
>
> **Third: even AI-to-AI handoff drops state.** Going from Claude Code to Claude Cowork — same vendor — loses context. Across vendors is worse. There is no shared memory layer.
>
> These aren't isolated annoyances. They're symptoms of something **structural**. Work is being distributed across humans and agents, across multiple vendors, some quick, some running overnight, with decisions made along the way. Each tool, on its own, only captures part of the picture. Linear sees engineering. Notion sees docs. Slack sees messages. Claude sees prompts. Synthesis falls on you — manually, after the fact, at high cognitive cost.
>
> **Blitzlist is the universal orchestration layer above all of them.** Items, documents, and files live in MCP — so your Claude Code session, your designer's Cowork, your client's claude.ai, and your team's web view all see the same source of truth. It tracks progress automatically. It draws your attention where it's needed. Persistent across sessions. Open-source. Self-hostable on Cloudflare in three minutes.
>
> The productivity stack was built before AI existed. **Blitzlist is the layer that was missing all along.**

## Reusable building blocks

These are the paragraph-sized components that compose the pitches. Drop them into any longer-form piece (blog post, deck, founder essay).

### The "Why universal" beat (~95 words, ~25 seconds)

The connective tissue between "lists matter" and "Blitzlist exists." Slots into any pitch as the bridge from problem to solution.

> Work is fragmenting differently in the AI era. The same task flows between humans and agents in an hour, across multiple vendors' tools, some finishing in seconds, others running overnight. Decisions are made mid-execution — and the next step depends on what was decided. No single tool captures the whole picture. Linear sees engineering. Notion sees docs. Slack sees messages. Claude sees prompts. Each gives you a slice. **Synthesis falls on the human**, who pays the cognitive tax of stitching it together — usually by hand, usually after the fact, usually losing fidelity along the way.
>
> **A universal list isn't a nice-to-have anymore. It's how you steer.**

### The "three frictions" passage

The visceral problem statement. Lead with this in technical audiences.

> If you've spent a week working with AI agents, you've felt three things break down.
>
> **First: your AI's memory dies.** Your Claude Code todo list is gone in 24 hours.
>
> **Second: your artifacts don't travel.** Screenshots, copy-paste, downloads, re-uploads. The work happens in the gaps between tools.
>
> **Third: AI-to-AI handoff drops state.** Even within one vendor's products, context doesn't follow you.

### The "each tool sees a slice" line

Six words that close a defensive flank. Without it, a skeptic thinks *"I just use Claude — it sees everything."* With it, you preempt that.

> *"Linear sees engineering. Notion sees docs. Slack sees messages. Claude sees prompts."*

Always include all four. The rhythm matters; dropping one breaks the symmetry.

## Craft notes (for whoever delivers these)

1. **"But not anymore" is a mic drop.** Pause before saying it. Don't soften it, don't paraphrase it, don't extend it. Three words that resolve every problem listed above. People will quote it back to you.

2. **"Universal orchestration tool / layer" is a category-defining phrase.** It positions Blitzlist as a *new layer of the stack* — above the tools, below the agents, where coordination actually happens. Use it consistently; resist substitutions.

3. **"Draws attention where it's needed" makes the Compass make sense.** Always pair the structural argument with a quick Compass demo. They amplify each other.

4. **Always end with the closing button.** Don't continue past it. Don't add caveats. The button is the line that survives the meeting.

5. **The personal arc is the strongest asset.** Boardroom → founder → product → AI → family. Investors hear professional pedigree. Builders hear technical credibility. Anyone hears the universality. Most founder stories pick one register; this one covers all four. Lead with A whenever you're given more than 30 seconds.
